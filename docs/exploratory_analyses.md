## Interpretations superseded in the main manuscript

- A scale endpoint is an observable response rule, not proof of a stored belief.
- A middle rating is not proof of ignorance; dividing ratings by ten does not
  establish calibrated subjective probabilities.
- A lower scale rate does not establish a more accurate misinformation prevalence.
  Stricter scoring can reduce false positives and increase false negatives.
- Response instability alone does not make an observed rate an upper bound on
  stored misinformation. Such an upper bound also needs a false-negative restriction.
- A smaller partisan gap under one format does not identify partisan guessing.
- Associations with engagement are descriptive, not effects of knowledge or
  misinformation on participation. Voting intention is not observed turnout.
- A mathematically identified latent response-class model does not establish that
  its classes correspond to knowledge, misinformation, tentative belief, or ignorance.
- These convenience-sample results do not establish that ignorance predominates
  in the public, or identify national misinformation prevalence.

These limits concern interpretation. The reconstructed numerical analyses remain
available, including historical key sensitivities, repaired missingness, and
optimization diagnostics. Nothing is silently relabeled as validation of the
new manuscript's premeasurement states.
